\begin{abstract}
In a synthetic twenty-site manufacturing network, a graph convolutional network
(GCN) corrects an eight-step planner's (H8) candidate costs using paired
Monte Carlo targets. A five-block, 120-world comparison separates operational,
learning, and graph effects.
The full controller reduces mean cost by 4.845\% relative to the MDL2 heuristic,
with 94.567 fewer patient losses and 110.942 more completions per world.
Relative to H8, cost falls by 2.136\% overall and is lower in every block mean,
but two conditions have additional patient losses. The matched
identity-adjacency comparison yields a smaller 0.407\% reduction, with a
descriptive 95\% interval of $[-0.087,1.041]\%$ and block disagreement.
A separate three-block, 24-world study removes global H8 from both models
and matches their bounded messages, parameter counts, and fitting budgets.
Here the GCN costs 4.843\% less than the non-graph model, with lower cost in
all three block means, but incurs 16.667 additional patient losses and
12.375 fewer completions per world. The primary study supports an operational
benefit and a learning-related cost increment; the message study supplies a
cost-only architecture signal. Neither establishes service-preserving
independent GCN gains or explains the different graph effects across these
distinct interfaces. Findings remain synthetic development evidence, not
clinical validation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Personalized manufacturing requires coordination between patient-specific demand
and distributed production resources. A specimen and its resulting product
cannot be reassigned to a different patient. Moving a specimen may make an idle
bioreactor accessible, but the delay can consume the patient's remaining service
window. Reagent transfers and bioreactor relocations also affect which patients
can begin production later. Existing work on cell-therapy supply networks and
dynamic capacity planning provides the application context~\cite{Wang2023,Tseng2025}.
The present study evaluates a synthetic decision model, not a clinically
validated prediction system.

The rationale for a graph representation is relational: the value of a request
at one site can depend on resources and competing needs at connected sites.
A specimen transfer, for example, is useful only if production resources become
available before its service window closes. Graph aggregation offers a way to
combine a candidate request with related operational states. It does not create
new observations or make physical transfers feasible. Its value must be tested
against a non-graph model that receives the same permitted information.

A central difficulty is attributing the performance of a composite controller.
A learned model can appear effective because it is coupled to a stronger
planner, and a graph model can appear effective because its comparator receives
less information. Neither comparison isolates the proposed contribution.
Cost introduces a second attribution problem: a controller may reduce procurement
or other operating expenses while losing more patients. Reporting only the
weighted objective can conceal that trade-off.

We therefore ask three separate questions. First, does the complete controller
improve on the original operational heuristic? Second, does learning improve on
the same planner without a learned correction? Third, does neural message passing
improve on a matched representation that retains the same inputs, local feature
projection, global readout, planning procedure, and training recipe? We report
cost, losses, completions, unfinished service, and adverse worlds for each
comparison. An aggregate improvement does not replace these service measures.

The method, identified as Localv2 in the implementation, learns a correction to
the cost difference between candidate first actions. It uses paired simulations
that run to the original endpoint under a frozen continuation policy. At
decision time, a shared eight-step planner supplies candidate scores and the
learned correction reranks them. Only the selected first action is executed
before replanning. This is simulation-feedback policy improvement with paired
Monte Carlo targets. It is not imitation of the planner's selected action,
a neural actor, or the earlier temporal-difference state-tail method.

The graph representation uses established graph-convolution operations~\cite{Kipf2017}
to combine candidate requests with operational features before producing a
scalar cost correction. Its matched Self ablation replaces only neural
adjacency with the identity. Both retain the shared planner and global readout.
A second, explicitly exploratory study asks whether graph mixing changes the
cost--service trade-off when both models instead receive identical bounded
neighbor messages and neither uses global H8. This is not a factorial removal
of H8 from the first experiment: model, training recipe, and evaluation panel
also differ. The two studies therefore answer complementary, not interchangeable,
questions about graph value.

The paper makes three contributions within these bounds:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A graph-aware candidate-cost controller couples short-horizon planning
with paired full-tail simulation feedback under the original twenty-site
physical rules. Its graph operations are established components; the specified
decision interface and target construction define the method studied here.
\item A five-block comparison separates the integrated operational effect,
the learned correction, and the marginal graph effect on the same world panel.
The first two yield cost gains; the graph increment over matched Self remains
small and unstable.
\item A matched bounded-message study identifies a cross-block graph cost
advantage accompanied by increased patient losses. Combined with service
accounting and retained negative studies, it distinguishes a cost-only
architecture benefit from a patient-protecting system improvement.
\end{enumerate}
The latter finding gives graph representation a substantive role in the
analysis without transferring its cost gain to the full Graph/H8 controller
or treating patients harmed by the policy as an acceptable unreported expense.

\section{Problem Formulation and Service Accounting}
\label{sec:problem}
\subsection{Network and physical constraints}
The benchmark has twenty physical manufacturing sites and $T=52$ decision
epochs. Geographic links, demand generation, production timing, supplier
disruptions, resource limits, patient dynamics, and cost coefficients remain
those of the original task. The neural representation contains an additional
virtual capacity hub, giving 21 representation nodes rather than 21 physical
manufacturing sites.

A waiting patient's intact specimen may make at most one qualified direct
transfer before manufacturing. Specimens cannot be split, pooled, reassigned,
or routed repeatedly. Reagents may move on qualified resource links, and idle
bioreactors may relocate on capacity links. An in-process patient batch cannot
move. Finished product remains associated with the same patient and returns to
the collection site. The primary conditions use a one-epoch specimen-availability
lead and zero-epoch finished-product return lead. These are benchmark assumptions,
not claims about feasible clinical transport.

Let $x_t$ denote the complete simulator state and $o_t$ the controller's public
observation. Public information includes inventories, production and transfer
pipelines, observed demand and history, supplier status, patient summaries, and
time. The deployed controller cannot use realized future demand, hidden patient
attributes, or future random draws. The observation and its encoded features
are summaries; we do not claim that they are sufficient statistics for the
complete latent state.

\subsection{Requests and finite-horizon cost}
The controller issues a group-major, eighty-coordinate request,
\begin{equation}
 a_t=(a_t^{\mathrm{s}},a_t^{\mathrm{r}},a_t^{\mathrm{b}},
      a_t^{\mathrm{q}})\in[-1,1]^{80},
 \qquad a_t^g\in[-1,1]^{20}.
 \label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
The groups request specimen transfer, reagent transfer, idle-bioreactor
relocation, and replenishment. Transfer coordinates express site-level net
requests, not arbitrary edge flows. The original decoder enforces availability,
quantity, integrality, connectivity, and patient-identity constraints.
Consequently, a proposed donor--receiver correction does not guarantee that the
native matching rule executes that exact edge movement.

The finite candidate bank restricts the implemented controller's search, not the
physical admissibility of the task. All three transfer channels and replenishment
remain available. Actions outside the bank were not searched by this method;
the experiment does not establish optimal control over the full request space.

The per-epoch cost retains the original decomposition,
\begin{equation}
 c_t=c_t^{\mathrm{operating}}+c_t^{\mathrm{patient\ loss}}
     +c_t^{\mathrm{expiry}}+c_t^{\mathrm{urgency}},
 \qquad r_t=-c_t,
 \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
and the objective is
\begin{equation}
 J(\pi)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}c_t\right].
 \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
The discount factor is one. Operating cost already includes charged transfers;
those components must not be added a second time. Cost is reported in modeled
weighted-objective units, not dollars or realized financial savings. No new
settlement phase, changed reward, or extended endpoint is introduced.

\subsection{Losses, completions, and unfinished service}
Let $L$ be cumulative patient losses, $D$ completed services, and $U_T$ active
unresolved patient obligations at the endpoint. Losses include the simulator's
recorded expiry and ineligibility events; completions require the modeled
patient service to finish. Unresolved service includes waiting patients,
patients in production, specimens in transit, and any active finished-product
or return obligations. It is not just the waiting queue. The recorded
\texttt{cutoff.terminal\_patients} field includes resolved outcomes and must not
be substituted for $U_T$.

For a panel with identical enrollment $N$ and exhaustive endpoint states,
patient accounting gives $N=L+D+U_T$. Thus candidate-minus-reference
differences satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \Delta D=-\Delta L-\Delta U_T.
 \label{eq:service}
\end{equation}
This accounting identity does not make any one outcome a sufficient service
criterion. Fewer losses can coexist with more unresolved obligations, and a
smaller queue can result from losses rather than successful service. We retain
all three outcomes and their observation horizon.

\section{Planning-Assisted Candidate-Cost Learning}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Shared H8 planner and structured candidates}
The shared planner, denoted H8, considers at most fourteen first-action
candidates. A deterministic generator uses public state and static physical
topology to construct original MDL2 and MDL3 anchors and structured corrections.
The corrections cover specimen movement, reagent movement, idle-capacity
movement, replenishment, and complementary resource changes. Shortlisting
uses public shortage, urgency, logistics, and diversity rules. It is not a
trained proposer or a search over arbitrary joint action sequences.

Candidate deduplication can reduce the bank size. It preserves equivalent
anchor behavior and does not refill an empty family's quota with more actions
from another family. The physical decoder, rather than the shortlist label,
determines realized flow. This generator differs from the earlier fixed
epsilon-template bank; the later results must not be attached to that older
candidate specification.

For $a\in\mathcal A(o_t)$, H8 evaluates the first request followed by original
MDL2 continuation in two sampled prediction futures for
$h_t=\min(8,T-t)$ epochs:
\begin{equation}
 H_t(a)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{2}
        \sum_{j=0}^{h_t-1}\widehat c_{t+j}^{(k)}(a,\mathrm{MDL2}).
 \label{eq:h8}
\end{equation}
There is no learned terminal value in this score. Prediction futures use the
existing public-information demand estimator and conditional patient sampler,
not the realized evaluation future. The same prediction context is reused
across candidates at a decision. Shared stream identities do not, by themselves,
prove that every modeled event remains pathwise aligned after decisions diverge.

Let $a_0$ be H8's selected anchor, including its existing tie rule. A learned
model supplies a correction to each candidate relative to this anchor:
\begin{equation}
 S_\theta(o_t,a)=H_t(a)+s\{f_\theta(X_t(a))-f_\theta(X_t(a_0))\},
 \qquad s=10^9.
 \label{eq:decision}
\end{equation}
The controller selects the smallest corrected cost and retains the anchor when
the improvement is no greater than $10^{-6}$ native-cost units. That tolerance
is a numerical selection rule, not a patient-safety margin. The system executes
only the selected first action, observes the next state, and replans.

\subsection{Paired Monte Carlo targets}
During a collection round, the continuation policy $\pi_r$ is frozen. At each
selected training state, the collector compares the H8 anchor with a distinct
alternative selected by the fixed collection rule. Each first action is
followed by $\pi_r$ through the original endpoint. Two native futures per action
supply paired full-tail costs. Denote their mean by $\overline G_{\pi_r,t}(a)$.
The fitted target is
\begin{equation}
 y_r(a,a_0)=
 \frac{\{\overline G_{\pi_r,t}(a)-\overline G_{\pi_r,t}(a_0)\}
       -\{H_t(a)-H_t(a_0)\}}{s}.
 \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
Lower values favor the alternative. The pair compares complete costs under one
declared continuation policy; it is neither a reward-sign advantage nor a full
action-value target added again to H8.

The learner minimizes centered mean squared error on the current round's pairs:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_r(\theta)=
 \frac{1}{|\mathcal B|}\sum_{(a,a_0,y)\in\mathcal B}
 \left[f_\theta(X_t(a))-f_\theta(X_t(a_0))-y\right]^2 .
 \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
The anchor output is centered by subtraction, not fitted as a separate absolute
cost. Prior-round buffers are not replayed. After the fixed updates, the new
model defines the next collection policy.

This procedure learns from simulated consequences of its actions rather than
from labels telling it to imitate H8. It is a planning-assisted form of
simulation-feedback policy improvement. Unlike the earlier TD8 state-value
method, it uses no bootstrapped learned terminal target in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:target}; all tails reach $T$. It also does not train a DDPG, PPO,
or SAC actor. References to a learning increment in this paper concern the
specific Graph/H8 versus H8 comparison, not all reinforcement-learning methods
or online deployment adaptation.

Training simulator snapshots and future outcomes supply labels and pairing
provenance only. They are not predictor features. Evaluation disables fitting
and does not expose those training-only outcomes to the controller.

\subsection{Graph mechanism and matched Self representation}
Both learned models receive $X_t(a)\in\mathbb R^{21\times41}$. The first
36 columns are the same public feature encoding. Four columns add the site's
specimen, reagent, idle-bioreactor, and replenishment requests; the virtual hub
receives zeros in these four columns. The final column repeats
$\{H_t(a)-H_t(a_0)\}/s$ across nodes. This score is a public-information planning
output, not an observed future cost.

The encoder graph merges the configured information, specimen, reagent, and
capacity relations into one undirected adjacency. In this representation,
capacity links use a virtual hub connected to all twenty sites; the hub carries
aggregate idle and total capacity, time, and its indicator. It is not a physical
transfer station. Physical transfer eligibility remains governed by the
simulator, independently of this computational graph. Facility-to-facility
weights decrease with configured geographic travel time, with a positive floor;
hub links have unit weight. Duplicate relations are merged rather than assigned
different learned message functions.

Writing the weighted adjacency as $A$, the encoder uses
$\widehat A=\widetilde D^{-1/2}(A+I)\widetilde D^{-1/2}$, where
$\widetilde D_{ii}=\sum_j(A+I)_{ij}$.
The graph backbone uses two width-64 layers. With the prescribed symmetric
normalized adjacency $\widehat A$ including self-loops,
\begin{equation}
 Z^{(0)}=X,\qquad
 Z^{(\ell+1)}=\relu\!\left(
 \widehat A[Z^{(\ell)}W_\ell+\mathbf1 b_\ell^\top]\right),
 \quad \ell=0,1.
 \label{eq:gcn}
\end{equation}
Localv2 adds a learned raw-feature projection before a flattened global readout:
\begin{equation}
 Z=\relu\!\left(Z^{(2)}+XW_{\mathrm{raw}}+
                    \mathbf1 b_{\mathrm{raw}}^\top\right),\qquad
 f_\theta(X)=w^\top\vecop(Z)+b.
 \label{eq:localv2}
\end{equation}
The scalar head is initially zero, so the initial corrected policy follows H8.

For a given candidate, each graph layer mixes transformed state and request
features before applying its nonlinearity. This permits a site's representation
to depend on its encoded neighbors' circumstances. The raw-feature skip retains
a direct local path, while the global head combines all node outputs. Two layers
also permit site-to-site paths through the hub. The model is therefore neither
a strictly local controller nor a learned communication protocol. Its proposed
advantage is a relational processing bias, not privileged or faster information.
The aggregate adjacency also does not separately model reagent, specimen, and
reactor interactions; successful cost prediction is not proof that any one
physical mechanism was learned.

Graph and Self each have 10,881 trainable parameters. Self replaces only
$\widehat A$ with $I_{21}$; its local projection, flattened head, feature inputs,
physical links, and shared planner remain unchanged. Within a training block,
trainable tensors and the initial optimizer and private batch-generator state
match. Physical isolation is not part of this ablation. Self can still combine
site information through its global readout and exploit the shared planner.
The comparison therefore estimates the marginal value of neural message passing
within this full controller.

The global head in Eq.~\ref{eq:localv2} is linear. With identity adjacency,
Self is a sum of nonlinear node-wise terms at the encoded-feature interface,
not an arbitrary nonlinear MLP over concatenated raw sites. Conversely, global
planning features can already encode joint decisions before this head. The
encoder has 36 facility links and 20 hub links, not a complete graph of 190
direct facility links. Two-hop reachability does not make Graph and Self
functionally equivalent. The fixed 21-node flattened readout also does not
provide arbitrary-size invariance.

\subsection{Training rounds and frozen evaluation}
The replication trains five Graph/Self pairs independently of the earlier
three-block panels. Each model collects 96 main training worlds in three rounds
of 32, using the fixed round-robin condition order. The collection recipe selects
three states per world and retains its original alternative-selection,
deduplication, and no-distinct-alternative skip rules. After each round it makes
1,024 Adam updates with 64 pairs per batch and learning rate $10^{-4}$.
Across ten models, the completed training record contains 960 main trajectories
and 30,720 updates. Counterfactual tails and planner predictions are additional
computation, not additional independent training worlds.

Matching initial tensors and stream allocations does not guarantee identical
on-policy data. Once decisions diverge, the two models can visit different
states and collect different pair-buffer sizes; subsequent batch indices can
also differ. The experiment preserves matched initialization and the same
sampling rule, not a false claim of identical realized samples throughout
training. All ten final models are sealed before evaluation. No intermediate
checkpoint is selected on evaluation performance, and evaluation performs zero
optimizer updates.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Planning-assisted paired cost-correction learning}
\label{alg:method}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Initialize Graph or Self with a zero-output correction head
\For{each of the three fixed policy rounds}
  \State Freeze the current continuation policy
  \State Collect 32 worlds in the fixed condition order
  \State At prescribed states, form an H8 anchor and distinct alternative
  \State Run paired native futures through $T$ under frozen continuation
  \State Form current-round residual labels using Eq.~\eqref{eq:target}
  \State Perform 1,024 centered-MSE updates using Eq.~\eqref{eq:loss}
\EndFor
\State Seal the final model before any evaluation
\For{each evaluation decision}
  \State Form public structured candidates and shared H8 scores
  \State Rerank with Eq.~\eqref{eq:decision}; retain the anchor on a tie
  \State Execute the first request only, with learning disabled
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

completed training trajectories, without new training, labels, seeds, or seals.
The original failed attempt remains in the record. The recovery's completed
comparison and locally verified archive define the main result panel; later
procurement preservation is a different experiment.

\subsection{Metrics, uncertainty, and fixed screens}
For candidate $c$ and reference $r$, reported cost saving is
\begin{equation}
 R_{c/r}=100\,\frac{\overline C_r-\overline C_c}{\overline C_r}.
 \label{eq:saving}
\end{equation}
It is the difference of mean costs divided by the reference mean, not the mean
of world-level percentages. Different contrasts have different denominators,
so their percentages are not additive. Patient deltas are candidate minus
reference: positive $\Delta L$ is adverse, positive $\Delta D$ is favorable,
and positive $\Delta U_T$ is adverse.

The saved analysis uses 2,000 paired hierarchical bootstrap resamples. It samples
five training blocks with replacement, keeps the four conditions equally
weighted, and samples six paired worlds within each selected block--condition.
The same resampled indices apply to all methods and contrasts. The percentile
95\% intervals are descriptive and not adjusted for multiplicity. They are
not confirmatory significance tests or clinical noninferiority intervals.

The fixed cost screen requires at least 0.5\% mean saving, positive saving in
every block, and a positive descriptive lower cost bound. For the primary
Graph/Self and Graph/H8 comparisons, patient protection additionally requires
nonpositive mean extra losses overall and in every condition, plus a
nonpositive descriptive upper patient-loss bound. The other saved comparisons
retain their recorded overall/condition mean patient guardrails. We reproduce
these screens as implemented rather than impose a new rule retrospectively.
Completions and unresolved service remain reported outcomes even where they
are not components of a particular fixed screen.

\subsection{Computational and preservation accounting}
Training, native evaluation, planning predictions, neural scoring, and elapsed
time are separate quantities. The evaluation recovery records 24,960 native
steps, 1,450,576 prediction steps, and 12,480 learned-model forward calls, with
zero evaluation updates. These are execution counts, not measures of statistical
sample size. The prediction burden can differ despite identical horizon and
candidate ceilings because states and deduplicated banks differ.

The complete evaluation phase took 3,217.136 seconds in the recorded run, and
analysis/archive took 534.398 seconds. Table~\ref{tab:compute} gives saved
method-level totals; summed trajectory times are not the wall time of the
parallel evaluation. No hardware-independent speedup or computational graph
advantage is inferred from these timings. The main local archive is recorded as
verified; this does not establish off-device backup, cloud synchronization, or
public availability.

\subsection{Overall operational benefit}

(Table~\ref{tab:cost}), with 94.567 fewer losses and 110.942 more completions

Graph/H8--MDL2 difference cannot be attributed wholly to the graph operator or
to learning. Moreover, 16 of 120 worlds have more losses under Graph/H8 than
MDL2 despite its favorable average and operational screen.

\section{Graph Value Under Matched Bounded Communication}
\label{sec:exploratory}
\subsection{A separate matched-information test}
A later experiment replaces the shared global H8 interface with a synthetic
one-round message interface. Its graph and non-graph message models each have
30,017 parameters and receive matched messages, static relations, initial
tensors, training data, and batch indices. It uses three training blocks and
24 paired worlds, not the five-block primary panel. The original Localv2 models
and H8 are unchanged by this experiment.

Each site keeps its own observation and receives twelve operational summaries
from each permitted neighbor, covering demand, waiting patients, inventories,
capacity, supplier status, forecasts, pipelines, and waiting-patient urgency
and transfer eligibility. The same fixed permission graph has 36 undirected
links. One communication round sends 72 packets of 64 bytes, or 4,608 modeled
bytes per cycle. The protocol additionally charges a 65,536-byte static
provisioning envelope per role and world; these are modeled communication
allowances, not measured network traffic. Both models receive the same static relation rows and the same
transcript on an identical state. There is no relaying, dynamic hub, global H8,
MDL2 advice, or access to unreceived remote dynamic fields. Physical links are
not removed by the communication restriction.

The two-layer message GCN mixes the permitted ego-graph features. Its matched
non-graph model uses identity mixing but retains a nonlinear processor of its
own features and the received-neighbor mean. Both have the same raw-feature
skip and nonlinear readout. Two computational layers do not imply two message
rounds. Six models are fitted to the same existing 96 collection worlds,
with 1,024 updates per model and matched initialization and batch indices.
The messages affect scoring, not the own-site candidate bank. Selected site
requests are assembled and executed by the unchanged physical matching rule.
Thus the comparison changes graph processing, not information access, candidate
availability, or communication budget; equal parameter counts do not establish
equal execution time.

\subsection{Cross-block cost advantage with patient harm}
The message GCN reduces cost by 4.843\% relative to the message MLP, with a
descriptive interval of $[3.417,6.101]\%$. All three block means and all four
condition means favor the GCN in cost (Table~\ref{tab:messageblocks}). Only one
of 24 worlds has higher GCN cost. This is stronger cross-block cost evidence
within its own protocol than the small Graph/Self increment in the primary
panel; it is not a statistical comparison of the two experiments' effects.

The patient result reverses that favorable interpretation. The GCN loses
16.667 more patients and completes 12.375 fewer services per world. Fourteen
of 24 worlds have more losses, and losses increase in every block mean.
The four condition loss differences are $-9.000$, $+69.333$, $+21.667$, and
$-15.333$. The descriptive extra-loss interval is $[-19.126,55.769]$; its
inclusion of zero is not evidence of patient equivalence. The fixed joint
screen fails. The supported finding is a cost-only graph-architecture signal
under matched finite messages, not a patient-protecting improvement.

\subsection{What the two interfaces do and do not establish}
The main panel tests a graph bias on top of a shared network-aware planner and
global readout. The message study tests graph mixing on identical bounded
transcripts without that global planner. Neither gives GCN more observations
than its matched comparator. The latter cost signal warrants reporting graph
structure as an empirically consequential design choice in that protocol,

The numeric proximity between this 4.843\% result and the primary 4.845\%

information interfaces, training blocks, and world panels. Neither percentage
can be substituted for the other or combined with the other's service outcomes.
The imposed message limit is also a synthetic design choice, not a measured
constraint from a manufacturing organization.

\section{Service Preservation and Negative Development Evidence}
\subsection{Procurement preservation within each policy}
A separate procurement study applies the same conservative cap to each frozen
message architecture. Its completed reexecution contains 24 paired worlds and
96 evaluations, comparing capped and uncapped policies within each architecture
and across architectures. The saved reconciliation reports preservation of each
architecture's own patient and physical trajectory, including the paired-state
checks, while procurement accounting changes.

Within the GCN policy, the cap reduces mean cost by 9.186\% with zero additional
losses and zero additional completions. Within the non-graph policy, it reduces
cost by 10.645\%, also with zero changes in those outcomes. The larger gain
belongs to the non-graph policy. These are common-rule procurement gains,
not new reinforcement-learning gains or graph-specific improvements.

With both architectures capped, the GCN still costs 2.940\% less but loses
37.625 more patients and completes 36.333 fewer services per world. Eighteen of
24 worlds have more losses. Its block loss differences are $-28.500$,
$+84.500$, and $+56.875$. Thus preserving each policy relative to itself does
not make the two policies equivalent in patient service.

The separate archive-only recovery closed local preservation without changing
these estimates. Failure and closeout records remain distinct in
\ref{app:evidence}; no training or evaluation was repeated for this closeout.

\subsection{Negative development screens}
Several bounded development studies tested explanations or remedies for the
remaining graph and service gaps. They are not pooled with the primary panel.
Table~\ref{tab:negative} summarizes their recorded decisions. A failed
implementation attempt is distinguished from a completed negative screen;
only completed comparisons support performance statements.

A procurement/allocation crossover worsened its own GCN baseline when GCN
procurement was combined with non-graph allocation. Deadline coordination
improved each family's own losses but retained the matched cross-family service
gap and increased pending service. The feasible-start selector had limited
action exposure and also retained the cross-family gap. Competitive allocation
reduced mean losses while increasing unfinished service and harming one
condition. The joint-service rule changed no action, so its baseline differences
cannot be credited to the intervention.

A training-only feasibility study subsequently examined direct loss and
pending-service targets without fitting a new model. Loss effects showed some
repeated-future stability, whereas the pending-service signal was weak and
inconsistent across blocks. This did not support preparation of a target-only
fit. It did not estimate conditional predictability from all available inputs,
compare new architectures, or prove that service learning is impossible.

The later common-joint-action comparison also failed to establish an
architecture gain: under matched information and a common continuation,
GCN cost was 0.0184\% higher, losses were 0.980 higher, and completions were
0.830 lower than the non-graph model. The GCN cost and service differences
were unfavorable in every block and condition mean. A representation-factorial
screen then localized improvements to one block rather than establishing a
cross-block repair. Neither supports adding a more complex encoder by default.

A conditional-recipient screen fitted nine short models and reused twelve
already-viewed development states, rather than evaluating complete new policies.
Relative to its non-graph comparator, the GCN increased cost by 0.0296\% while
reducing losses by 2.063 and increasing completions by 2.333; GATv2 increased
cost by 0.0477\%, reduced losses by 1.292, and increased completions by 1.458.
The fixed screens failed. Lower candidate regret in this small menu did not
translate into the required cost--service result. Subsequent training-data
inspection found no stable missed beneficial forced choice in the viewed
development menu. A multi-resource inventory found public reagent/bioreactor
co-pressure in zero, eight, and zero of eight inspected roots across the three
blocks. This narrow support does not justify claiming a cross-block mechanism
or automatically starting another fit.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\subsection{What the comparisons establish}
The integrated method improves on MDL2 in mean cost and patient outcomes on the
primary development panel. H8 and Self/H8 also improve on MDL2, so the full
effect is a property of the controller and its comparison, not a direct estimate
of the value of graph learning. Graph/H8 improves the shared H8 cost in every
block, but its condition-level patient harms prevent an unconditional service
claim. The graph-versus-Self point estimate is favorable and small, with
uncertainty and block disagreement that fail the fixed screen.

\subsection{Graph structure as a conditional design choice}
The matched message experiment provides a positive graph finding that should
not be erased by its failed joint screen: GCN lowers cost in all three block
means under the same permitted messages, parameter count, and fitting opportunity.
It also increases mean losses in all three blocks. Graph structure can therefore
change the achieved cost--service trade-off in this protocol without producing
a patient-protecting improvement. Neither ``graph structure is useless'' nor
``graph structure makes the system better overall'' follows from these results.

In the primary controller, shared H8 and the flattened global readout may leave little additional value for
neural message passing. This is a plausible explanation, not an identified
mechanism or a reason to deprive Self of information. The comparison holds the
intended architecture contract fixed while allowing on-policy data to differ.
It does not isolate the graph operator under identical realized training
examples, and the observed gap can include the downstream consequences of
different policies.

The practical question is consequently narrower than whether a GCN can beat
an information-poor baseline: can a relational representation improve decisions
over a matched information processor while retaining service performance?
The present studies establish a cost-only signal in one interface and a small,
uncertain increment in the other. A new representation or hybrid decision
mechanism would need its own matched comparison; it cannot inherit the primary
controller's patient gains or the message model's cost reduction.

Table~\ref{tab:position} states the supported graph claims at the interfaces
where they were measured. These are observed protocol-specific results, not
identified causal conditions under which GCN must succeed. In particular,
bounded information, a different fitting recipe, and a different action
interface changed together between studies.

\subsection{Why cost and patient service diverge}
The objective combines several components and is not a hard patient-protection
constraint. A policy can reduce one operating component while changing which
patients receive timely resources. Conversely, a public rule can remove
unneeded procurement without changing that policy's patient path. The latter
is useful engineering evidence, but it cannot repair a service difference that
already exists between two policies.

Endpoint accounting is equally important. A patient who remains active at
epoch 52 has neither completed service nor necessarily suffered a recorded
loss. Removing such patients from the service comparison would make deferral
look better than it is. We therefore retain the original endpoint and report
pending obligations instead of adding a post hoc settlement period or
noninferiority tolerance. The competitive-allocation results and training-only
label-feasibility findings
reinforce the need to distinguish loss prevention from completed service.

\subsection{External validity and statistical limits}
All observations are synthetic. Patient deterioration, demand regimes,
disruptions, resource costs, and transport assumptions have not been validated
here against clinical outcomes or a deployed manufacturing network. Results
cannot establish patient safety, real monetary savings, or performance under
an organization's actual communication restrictions.

Five training blocks provide more information about training variability than
additional evaluation worlds on the same trained pair, but they remain a small
basis for generalization. The intervals are descriptive and unadjusted, the
condition set is fixed, and the results have been examined during development.
Passing a screen is not confirmation on an untouched external dataset.
The exploratory three-block studies are still more limited and must not be
used to strengthen the main estimate by pooling selected outcomes.

The structured bank also limits the method's action support. It does not search
all admissible flows or future action sequences. Prediction futures and
training tails introduce Monte Carlo uncertainty; matched stream identities
are not proof of event-by-event equality. None of the saved diagnostics uniquely
separates representation loss, stochastic continuation, and optimization error.
Negative results constrain the tested recipes, not the entire class of graph
models or patient-service objectives.

\subsection{Generalization to simulation uncertainty}
The four primary regimes also occur during training; testing new random worlds
from those regimes is not evidence of broad out-of-distribution generalization.
The supplementary transfer and stale-advice tests in
\ref{app:robustness} cover particular perturbations and retain negative outcomes.
They do not establish robustness to arbitrary changes in deterioration,
manufacturing duration, supplier reliability, transport lead time, or the
dependence between demand and disruptions.

A prospective uncertainty study would first separate parameter uncertainty
within a calibrated model from distribution shift and model misspecification.
It would distinguish frozen-policy transfer from retraining, prespecify
application-supported ranges, keep information and computation matched, and
report patient losses and unresolved service alongside cost. Changing many
settings until a favorable graph result appears would not answer this question.
No such study is launched or claimed as completed in this revision. The current
evidence leaves this collaborator-prioritized research question open.

\subsection{Relation to the earlier development record}
The repository retains earlier actor--critic DDPG and state-value TD/CEM
implementations. DDPG~\cite{Lillicrap2016}, temporal-difference
learning~\cite{Sutton1988}, value-augmented model predictive
control~\cite{Hansen2022}, and CEM~\cite{DeBoer2005} describe different
methodological components in that history. They are not alternative names for
the paired residual estimator used here.

Earlier positive operational findings, inconclusive learning attribution,
historical Flat comparisons, and adverse sensitivity results remain in their
original records. They are not inserted into this panel or used as evidence
for a new graph contribution. Likewise, changing the labels from losses and
completions to losses and pending service supplies no new information by itself.
A new objective weighting or input representation would be a new intervention
requiring its own mechanism and prospective assessment.

The earlier three-block development results motivated the five-block
replication; they did not follow an unexamined confirmatory selection process.
The original five-block protocol was frozen before evaluation. A checkpoint
cost-scale integer/float equivalence error stopped the first attempt before
evaluation; the authorized recovery retained the original 120 world identities
and completed only their missing evaluation, without selecting favorable prior
test outcomes. This procedural recovery does not remove earlier model-selection
or development-panel uncertainty.

Historical reporting counted five scoped Native campaign designs and fourteen
configured learning arms, but those counts are not an exhaustive accounting of
the project's learning-rate, architecture, target, and follow-up choices.
An exact full-project selection count remains unresolved. Consequently, none
of the reported intervals should be interpreted as correcting for the complete
research search. \ref{app:history} retains the historical Flat evidence
instead of treating Self as a replacement that erases it.

\section{Conclusion}
The complete Graph/H8 controller improves on MDL2 in mean cost and patient
outcomes on the five-block synthetic development panel. It also supplies a
repeatable block-level cost increment
over the same H8 planner, but not patient protection in every condition.
The additional value of graph message passing over matched Self remains small
and unstable in this strong-planner interface.

The separate matched-message study reveals a clearer graph cost effect:
4.843\% lower mean cost and cost reductions in all three blocks, accompanied by
16.667 additional patient losses per world. This places graph representation
at the center of a conditional design finding, not a claim of universal
superiority. The later procurement result further shows that preserving each
policy's own service does not establish equivalence between architectures.
Together, the studies specify where the tested graph models add cost value and
where that value fails to protect service. All findings remain synthetic
development evidence, not clinical readiness or a proven service-preserving
independent GCN gain.

planning interface. Historical Flat instead uses a globally informed MLP and
answers a practical alternative-architecture question. Its stronger cross-site
